How to use blep

blep does two things with the Bluetooth Low Energy signals already around you: it walks you to a device you've lost, and it watches for trackers following you. No map, no account — everything happens on your phone.

🐾 Find a lost thing

Open blep and pick the device from the nearby list — keys with a tag, earbuds, a speaker, a watch, your car, anything that advertises Bluetooth. Tap the ✎ to give it a friendly name, or the ★ to keep a favourite handy. Paired devices that aren't advertising live sit under the Paired button.

A phone's Bluetooth antenna is roughly omnidirectional, so raw signal tells you how far but not which way. blep makes it directional using your own body:

  1. 1.Calibrate. Hold the phone flat against your chest and stand still for a moment. Your torso absorbs the signal from behind, so it reads strongest toward what's in front of you.
  2. 2.Sweep. Turn slowly in place. blep warms up as you face the device and cools as you turn away — stop where it's warmest.
  3. 3.Walk. Walk forward in that direction. The big arrow and the background colour (cool → warm) track every step; a trail shows where you've already searched.
  4. 4.Pinpoint. Up close, slow down and search low. Optional sound and haptics pulse faster as you close in — like a Geiger counter — and blep celebrates when you've found it.

Tip: chest-height, phone flat, move smoothly. Bluetooth is noisy and bounces off walls, so blep is a guide, not a GPS dot — it gets you the last room or two, then the warmer/colder feel does the rest. With motion sensors it also draws a small radar and gives turn-by-turn hints ("turn 30° left · ~8 m"); the same hunt runs on Apple Watch and Wear OS.

🛡 Is something tracking you?

Tap "Is something tracking you?" on the main screen. blep scans the same Bluetooth advertisements for a tracker travelling with you — an AirTag, Tile, Samsung SmartTag, or a Find My / Google Find My Device beacon someone may have slipped into your bag, coat or car. Keep the screen open for a minute while you move around.

It looks for a few things at once:

  • • Known trackers advertising in "separated from owner" mode close to you.
  • • Rotating IDs. A privacy tracker changes its Bluetooth ID to stay anonymous — but it gives itself away by reappearing at the same close range again and again.
  • • Following across contexts. With "remember across sessions" on, a tag that keeps turning up as your surroundings change (home, then transit, then somewhere new) is flagged — a device you merely pass daily on the same commute is not.

When something is flagged you'll see a card with two actions:

  • • Find it — because blep is a finder, it doesn't just warn: it walks you to the tracker with the same warm/cold guidance so you can locate and remove it.
  • • It's mine — mark your own tag so it stops being flagged.
Sensitivity
Relaxed, Balanced or Strict — how eagerly it flags. Use Relaxed in crowded places, Strict somewhere unfamiliar.
Background watch
Optionally keep an eye out while blep is closed and get a notification — on a battery-friendly schedule.
Location-aware
Off by default. If you turn it on, a coarse on-device place helps tell a real follower from your daily crowd — it never leaves your phone.

⚙ Settings

Open Settings from the ⚙ on the main screen. Everything is optional and on-device:

  • • Connected-device signal — show live signal bars for already-paired devices (uses a little more battery).
  • • Tracking sound — the audible warmer/colder tone while hunting.
  • • Show unnamed devices — include devices that don't advertise a name.
  • • Remember across sessions — the on-device log that powers "following across contexts" (no identity, no raw location).
  • • Location-aware detection — opt-in coarse location for the safety scan only (never the finder).
  • • Detection sensitivity and background scanning (keep scanning when minimized / scan on a timer, with an interval).

FAQ

Can blep find something across town?
No. It works within Bluetooth range — roughly a room, a floor, or a parking lot. It's not a GPS/cloud network like Find My; it guides you the last stretch by signal.
Does it need the internet or an account?
No accounts, no ads, no analytics, no internet. Your Bluetooth scans and motion sensing never leave the phone. See the privacy policy.
Why didn't it flag a tracker I know is there?
Give it a minute and move around — detection relies on seeing a device persist as you go. Try the Strict sensitivity. Some trackers only enter "separated" mode after they've been away from their owner for a while.
It flagged my own tag.
Tap It's mine on the alert — it won't bother you about that one again.
Is my phone supported?
Android and iPhone, plus companion apps for Apple Watch and Wear OS. blep is free and open source.
